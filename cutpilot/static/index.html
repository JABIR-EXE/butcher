FILE RULES · READ BEFORE UPLOADING

What goes in each row?

Automatic match + manual mapping
Product catalogue

One row = one product you sell. Include its SKU, category, stock unit, purchase cost, selling price and current opening stock. Import this first; update when products or costs change.

Required: Product name. Recommended: SKU, stock unit, purchase cost, selling price, opening stock.
Sales history

One row = one product line on a receipt. If a customer buys three different products, export three item rows, preferably sharing the same receipt ID. Do not upload only a daily total.

Required: Sale date, quantity, and product name or SKU. Recommended: unit price or line total and receipt ID.

Units must match the product catalogue (for example, kg must match kg). Do not mix kg and pieces in one product. Complex vendor-specific XML may need conversion to the included simple XML template.

A SIMPLE DAILY ROUTINE

How to use CutPilot.

A practical guide for the shop owner and staff. Follow these steps in order.

The simple rule

Products and costs are set up once. Sales are imported regularly. Stock is updated when deliveries arrive, waste is recorded, and CutPilot prepares a suggested buying list.

01

Set up your product list

Go to Import data → Step 1 and upload the product CSV, Excel or XML template, or use Add product in Products & stock for one item. Enter each meat product, its SKU, category, stock unit, purchase cost, selling price, minimum stock and opening stock.

  • Use kg for products sold by weight.
  • Enter the real cost you pay per kg, not the selling price.
  • Set the current stock quantity.
  • Use one consistent SKU per product.
Unknown products found in sales imports are created with zero cost and stock. Edit them before relying on profit or reorder calculations.
02

Record sales every day

At the end of each day, export item-level sales from your existing till/POS as CSV, Excel or supported XML and upload it in Import data. CutPilot suggests column matches; review or change them before confirming.

  • Required: sale date, quantity, and product name or SKU.
  • Recommended: unit price or line total, plus a stable receipt/sale ID.
  • Check the preview and correct missing products or units.
  • Use the same date and quantity units as your product catalog.
If your till cannot export files, create a spreadsheet with one row per product sold. The sample template shows the required columns.
03

Update stock when deliveries arrive

Open Inventory → Adjust stock and choose “Receive stock”. Enter the quantity received and optional expiry date. This creates a stock batch and movement record.

  • Do not add an incoming order to stock before it physically arrives.
  • Use “Set physical count” when doing a stocktake.
  • Record discarded meat in Waste monitor so stock and waste cost stay accurate.
04

Understand your money

The dashboard shows sales revenue. Estimated gross profit is revenue minus the product cost currently saved in CutPilot.

  • Revenue is the money recorded from sales, before expenses.
  • Estimated gross profit subtracts estimated product cost only.
  • It is not net profit: rent, wages, utilities, tax and other expenses are not included.
  • Keep product purchase costs updated for a better estimate.
05

Know what to buy

Open Purchasing → Recalculate forecast. Review each suggested quantity, supplier, delivery time and estimated cost.

  • Add suppliers and link them to products.
  • Check pack sizes and minimum order quantities.
  • Select the items and create draft purchase orders.
  • Approve, send to supplier yourself, then record the received quantities.
CutPilot does not automatically contact suppliers or place orders.
06

Ask the assistant

Use Ask CutPilot for questions such as:

  • “What were my sales this week?”
  • “Which products sold the most?”
  • “What should I order next week?”
  • “Which products are out of stock?”
  • “How much waste did I record?”
Answers are based on the data recorded in your local workspace.
